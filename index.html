Aug 2025 - May 2026 • CSUF

Solid-State Hydrogen-Powered Speedboat

This project explored the feasibility of using a solid-state hydrogen fuel cell as a clean energy source for a radio-controlled speedboat prototype. The goal was to design and integrate a modular hydrogen-electric propulsion system within a custom 3D-printed hull while balancing performance, weight, cost, and energy efficiency. I took a leading role in the project by guiding the system design process, coordinating the redesign decisions, and constructing the working prototype. Despite delays during the design phase, the team successfully delivered a functioning prototype for the ECS Project Innovation showcase. Water testing demonstrated that the boat was buoyant, maneuverable, and capable of reaching an estimated top speed of 6 mph while powered by the hydrogen fuel cell system. Although water leakage caused the main motor to degrade after initial testing, the project successfully demonstrated the potential of solid-state hydrogen fuel cells in small-scale marine propulsion applications. The final prototype provided valuable insight into the engineering tradeoffs associated with hydrogen-powered systems and identified future improvements, including better waterproofing, hull optimization, and the eventual integration of onboard hydrogen production.
